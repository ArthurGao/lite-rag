# Pour-over coffee at home

## Equipment

A pour-over needs a dripper, a paper filter, a kettle with a narrow spout and a scale. The narrow spout matters because it lets you pour slowly and in a controlled circle. A burr grinder gives more even particles than a blade grinder, and even particles extract evenly.

## Ratio and grind

Start with a ratio of 1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water. For one cup that is 15 grams of coffee and 240 grams of water. Grind to the size of coarse sea salt; if the drink tastes sour, grind finer next time, and if it tastes bitter, grind coarser.

## Water temperature

Use water between 92 and 96 degrees Celsius, just off the boil. Water that is too cool under-extracts and tastes flat; water that is boiling can scorch light roasts.

## The bloom

Pour about twice the weight of the coffee in water, then wait 30 seconds. Fresh coffee releases carbon dioxide during this bloom, and letting the gas escape helps the rest of the water flow through evenly.

## Total brew time

Pour the rest of the water in slow circles. The whole brew should finish in about three minutes. A brew that drains much faster was ground too coarse.
